What they are about

Bills by issue area · all 32

Half the docket is immigration and voting.

17 of 32 bills are about immigration (9) or voting and elections (8). Both are about who counts as part of a community and who gets to decide for it. The other issue areas are real, but this is where the volume is.

The punishment is the newer part, and it is spreading.

Six bills do not just block a local law — they go after the officials. Three carry criminal penalties, two withhold funding, and two create private enforcement: any citizen can sue to enforce the preemption. That last one is the SB 8 bounty mechanism migrating into preemption law, and almost nobody is counting it.

A takeover is not one bill. It is a maintenance schedule.

Missouri’s seizure of the St. Louis police department is three bills across two sessions — HB 495 to take it, then HB 2884 and HB 3066 to keep it running. Count only the headline bill and you miss two thirds of the work.

A bill dying is not the same as you being safe.

Five of these died in committee. That is exactly why this tracker counts attempts. Model language that fails in one state in one session reappears in four more the next, usually word for word.

What this measures

A bill enters this tracker when it is introduced — which means the tracker counts attempts, not just successes.

That is deliberate. A bill that dies in one state in one session is a bill that gets introduced in four more states next session, usually with the same language. Watching what is tried is how you see the strategy before it lands on you.

We focus on states where we have partners on the ground. The Local Solutions Support Center tracks this nationally and we work alongside them. What we add is the racial-control analysis and the communities on the receiving end.
