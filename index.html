Advancement Project

Stolen power.Take it back.

Across the country, state governments are taking away the right of Black and brown communities to govern themselves. They take over our schools. They seize our police departments and our water. They overrule what we voted for.

This* is a 150-year-old strategy being deployed more aggressively than ever before. But communities are fighting back.

Watch video → Learn more → Take action →

If they are stealing your power, you should know it and be able to name it.

“Preemption.” It’s what lawyers and legislators call the long-standing, racist practice of stealing local power.

It’s a real word for a real legal tool. But legalese works like a wall. The more neutral or dull sounding the name, the easier it is to overlook. And the longer it survives.

So we call it what it is: Stolen Power.

Timeline · 150 years of stolen power

What’s happening now is not new.

After the Civil War, Black communities built schools, elected officials, wrote local law, and cared for each other. Within a decade, white paramilitary coalitions calling themselves “Redeemers” took back the state legislatures and used them to abolish those local governments entirely.

The tool they used is the tool being used now. Just more widely than ever before.

They did not have to beat those communities at the local level. They moved the decision somewhere those communities could not reach.

Tracker · what's moving now

Happening right now, in statehouses, on paper.

In one legislative session, 42 states introduced more than 650 bills to strip power from cities and counties.

Search by state, by issue, or by what's about to move. A bill enters the tracker when it is introduced — so this counts attempts, not just successes. A bill that dies in one state this session is a bill that gets introduced in four more next session, usually word for word.
